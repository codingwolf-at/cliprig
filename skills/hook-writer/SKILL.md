# Hook writer

The hook is the text box in the first 2.6 seconds of the clip, and the first line of the post caption. It decides whether people stay.

## 1. Read first

- The clip's transcript lines (`jobs/<id>/candidates.md` or the clip's times in `transcript.json`). The hook must match what the clip actually shows and says.
- `learning.md` Current Rules: hook styles that won or lost on this page. Prefer what won.
- `log.md`: hooks used in the last 7 days. Don't reuse one.
- The campaign brief in `campaigns.md`: banned claims, required tone or names.

## 2. Write 3 options

Sentence case. 3 to 9 words, so it fits the hook box. Each option uses a different pattern:

- Situation: "When the guest corrects the host on his own show"
- Shock fact: "He turned down $2 million on air"
- Negation: "Nobody talks about this part of founding"
- Question: "Would you take this deal?"
- Number promise: "3 mistakes every new runner makes"
- Name + reaction, when the person is well known: "Even [name] didn't see this coming"
- Setup line for a punchline (stand-up): set up the bit without giving away the joke

## What doesn't work

- Bare labels: "Podcast clip", "Funny moment", "[Show] highlights"
- "Wait for it", "You won't believe this", "Watch till the end"
- Fake urgency, CAPS everywhere, more than one emoji

## Rules

1. The clip must deliver the hook. If it says 3 things, show 3. No clickbait.
2. Facts and numbers must be true and match the clip. Never put words in someone's mouth: no quotes they didn't say, no made-up opinions or context.
3. Don't give away the payoff. The hook opens the question; the clip answers it.
4. Never pose as the creator, show or brand. No "official".
5. Nothing on the playbook's skip list, and no medical, financial or legal claims.

Return the 3 options, mark your pick, and say in one line why it fits this clip.
